## A. Implementation summary

The previous Dalaran view reused the last Campaign run, shared a map baseline across classes, and could fill the baseline automatically. Upgrade plans ranked a single listed damage attribute. Advanced commands exposed manual gear, quote, and map entry.

The new planner consumes the current world's `AZSZONE` and `AZSARMOR` events. A player explicitly saves the actual loadout and map as a class-specific 100% baseline. The planner shows separate output and survival pressures and uses the larger value for its search objective. Plan A includes eligible current-expansion drops as conditional replacements. Plan B keeps equipped pieces and tiers and considers star upgrades. Both plans can populate the independent Equipment Simulator. The native character panel retains its **Upgrades** entry and appends single-item comparisons on hover. The planner also opens through `/sui gear` or SurvivorUI Global settings.

The implementation preserves the native gear formula, the checked per-star price calculation, the public map coefficients, set thresholds, and the native character panel post-hook. Existing card, tracker, and diagnostic features stay on their own paths. The normal workflow drops manual equipment and price editing, old snapshots as fallbacks, refresh controls, manual map selection, and the corresponding old slash commands. Legacy settings are kept on disk. Only the explicit save action writes the new baseline.

The independent runtime checks confirm that a live Campaign supplies map, tier, and equipment together, while Dalaran supplies current equipment and its own hub zone. A Campaign therefore offers the full workflow when the player saves a comparable class baseline. Dalaran opens directly into the simulator and keeps equipment attributes, gold quotes, set changes, and single-item previews available. The Campaign planner, baseline action, unavailable pressure rows, and plan controls are suppressed in the hub. Account ascension inputs remain unavailable through the inspected World interface, so the next-tier preview evaluates a zero-star drop. Game research and continuous data collection remain separate.

The correction round makes the theoretical scope visible, reevaluates saved inputs across compatible changes, separates known mode restrictions from affix qualifications, and freezes the zone context at its event boundary. Actual equipment, candidate generation, simulation, and tooltips share one class/slot/tier/star contract. The search uses legal seeds and stronger bounds within the original node budget. Simulator and plan icons now compare their own equipment contexts.

## C. Formulas, assumptions, and evidence

| Quantity | Calculation or rule | Evidence type and scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Piece attribute | `(slot base × tier slot scale + class profile base × tier profile scale) × common factor × (1 + min(stars, steps) × star_step + top-star bonus)` | Public gear formula and installed `AZS_GEN.pieces` and `gearTiers` definitions. Flat health and regeneration are absolute units. Percentage attribute differences are percentage points. The model sums before rounding. Native item display can round individual pieces. |
| Incremental star price | `floor(target star base × tier multiplier + 0.5)` | Derived from the checked November 16, 2026 price schedule. Bases for stars 1–5 are `408, 816, 1496, 2720, 4080` and already include the reduction. Multiple steps sum individually rounded quotes. Weapon parity was user-confirmed. Native factors cross-check Exotic `5.8`, Mythic `7.2`, and Elementium `7.6`. Exotic, Elementium, and the fifth Legendary star are formula estimates. |
| Set thresholds | Four matching armor pieces at 1+ stars, eight at 3+ stars, and eligible effects empowered with eight pieces at 5 stars | Native `setRules` and `setBonuses`. Verified numerical gold, pickup, Echo, and critical-damage modifiers contribute to attribute totals. Conditional, skill-specific, or unresolved effects remain qualitative. |
| Map output demand | `health_factor × Campaign tier health multiplier` | Public zone data and previously checked standard Campaign scaling. Normal through Heroic V health multipliers are `1, 1.79, 1.97, 2.16, 2.45, 2.72`. |
| Map survival demand | `first boss base hit × Campaign tier hit multiplier` | Public boss data and previously checked standard Campaign scaling. Hit multipliers are `1, 1.65, 1.77, 1.88, 2.01, 2.16`. The first boss hit is a representative early hit. |
| Output capability | `1 + sum of gear damage fractions` | Modeling assumption. The reference ability receives ordinary gear damage and non-gear effects are held neutral. |
| Survival capability | `(class starting HP + flat gear HP) / (1 − min(gear reduction, 0.60))` | Derived effective-health proxy using class data and the standard mitigation cap. Non-equipment modifiers and cap exceptions such as Warrior Titan are outside the proxy. |
| Normalized pressure | `100 × target demand / baseline demand × baseline capability / target capability` | Derived independently for output and survival. Values within `0.000001` percentage points of 100% normalize to 100%. |
| Combined difficulty | `max(output pressure, survival pressure)` when both exist | Product decision that preserves the limiting dimension. A partial estimate remains separate. |

The pressure estimates describe early equipment demand. They have no empirical interpretation as clear probability or overall DPS. They exclude enemy density, encounter scripts, affixes, challenge scaling, talents, critical chance and damage, cooldowns, Echo, regeneration, movement, area, summons, and growth routes from the pressure conversion. The planner shows scope qualifications next to the results and exposes formulas and exclusions under the expandable calculation-model section. The public map balance snapshot is `21071e86fee66ca7`. ## D. Optimization correctness and product decisions

Plan A enumerates known Campaign drops in the target expansion using the zone-pair rule, explicit overrides, and the native extra tiers for the expansion's final pair of maps. The candidate space includes upgrades to already worn pieces, including an actual item the player brought from another expansion. Replacement costs show calculable stars from the zero-star drop state and list acquisition separately. Plan B holds the slot and quality of each equipped item fixed. The account gold balance is outside both candidate filters. The simulator supports hypothetical choices outside either plan and distinguishes unknown acquisition costs from calculable stars. Downgrades have no assumed refund.

Incremental depth-first search first checks the actual setup and the highest quoted same-tier setup. It then constructs a legal full seed with protection-aware suffix bounds. A feasible seed can be reduced in cost before enumeration. These steps improve the incumbent and leave the proof to the search. A current estimate at or below 100% returns the actual configuration for zero gold. A feasible improvement minimizes comparable gold at or below 100%. When no candidate meets that target, the search minimizes remaining combined pressure and uses gold as the secondary criterion within the numerical tolerance. The largest per-slot damage spread is resolved first, with native slot order for ties. Choices retain deterministic cost, tier-rank, and star ordering.

Dominance requires the same acquisition path and protected-set contribution, at least as much Damage, HP and Damage Reduction, and no more gold. Suffix capacity bounds maximize the three attributes independently while accounting for the remaining pieces needed for each protected threshold. Independent maxima may combine incompatible choices, so they can only overstate actual capacity. Unquantified combat set thresholds remain explicit constraints. Quantified set effects leave the three search inputs unchanged.

The relaxed minimum-gold bound starts at the sums of the best zero-gold attributes in each remaining slot and applies the best possible gain per gold to a remaining deficit. It bounds three independent requirements: gear damage, `HP + required EHP × armor`, and the HP necessary at the standard armor cap. The two survival inequalities follow directly from capped mitigation. The maximum of the three optimistic cost bounds is still a lower bound. The price bound also applies to an unreachable pressure tie when the root capacity bound and a legal incumbent agree within tolerance. The eligible tie range stays anchored to the root bound.

Each plan visits at most 150,000 nodes. The seed and suffix work yield alongside enumeration. Completed searches establish the optimum only for included and comparable candidates. A budget cutoff leaves optimality and, where applicable, reachability unproven. When prices are missing, the UI distinguishes what the priced options establish from what unpriced candidates might achieve. The test oracles independently enumerate 80 stars-only and two mixed-tier cases using the full evaluator. Focused fixtures also cover a conditional zero-star Exotic replacement, competing protected four-piece thresholds, capped survival, no-upgrade and incomplete outcomes, and repeated deterministic results.

## E. Native API investigation and unresolved limitations

1. **World map and tier.** The public World API documents `zone`, `dt`, and `heroic` in `AZSZONE`. Installed `Run.lua` replaces `AZS_ZONE` using the native parser. The World `Hub.lua` account handler covers hub interactions. The inspected API and Lua do not expose a dedicated query for lobby selection. The documented read-only `sync` resends the World state. See the two runtime observations below for the distinction between a Campaign and the Hub. [Addon API](https://azreborn.com/guides/addon-api)
2. **Direct ascension and collection.** The hero menu is in a separate Glue Lua environment. World `AZSARMOR` in `Units.lua` only describes equipped slots. Proving Ground equipment exposed through `AZS_PG` represents a practice setup. It cannot establish the live collection or completed maps. The next-expansion final-map rule for Legendary and higher ascension was reported by the user and lacks independent server verification here. Direct ascension awaits readable account eligibility, a reliable quote, and the resulting star-state contract. The next-tier preview therefore uses a hypothetical zero-star drop with a separate acquisition explanation. [Gear progression](https://azreborn.com/guides/progression-and-cosmetics#gear-stars-and-sets)
3. **Empty equipment.** The native addon notes that a fully unequipped hero may receive no `AZSARMOR` message. Explicit empty data is valid. Silence keeps the state waiting and supplies no basis for inferring an empty loadout. When equipment is reported, the planner assumes it represents the complete worn list.
4. **Catalog and model coverage.** Class weapon layout uses native layout metadata where available, then native weapon lists and the established stock layout. Actual data cannot expand the class's slot whitelist. A class without a pack-tier weapon override uses the documented Legendary appearance fallback. An explicit incomplete per-tier weapon list provides insufficient evidence for that slot's drop, so Plan A excludes it. A class-valid simulator configuration can still show its theoretical benefit. Special set and skill interactions remain qualitative. [Content reference](https://azreborn.com/guides/content-reference)
5. **Native tooltip.** `Inspect.lua` establishes the order of `RefreshDoll`, named slot textures and their parent frames, native `OnEnter`, and `GameTooltip` ownership. The selected approach appends to that tooltip using the existing post-hook. Simulator and plan icons also expose the read-only comparison. Available evidence has not established a need for a companion-tooltip fallback. Real placement and text wrapping remain pending. [Addon API](https://azreborn.com/guides/addon-api)

6. **Modifiers and encounter identity.** The documented `affixes` feed is a comma-separated names list. Equivalent name lists are normalized as values. The legacy `aff` signal alone is not evidence of an active named modifier. Heroic tiers add built-in rules. The UI identifies the baseline, target, or both when the comparison omits extra rules. Explicit unsupported modes suppress the pressure and plans. The standard Campaign estimate excludes Rift and Endless encounters. The documented feed lacks reliable mode identity for every context. [Addon API](https://azreborn.com/guides/addon-api) [Heroic tiers and modes](https://azreborn.com/guides/runs-and-zones#difficulty-tiers)
7. **Session identity.** The analyzer stores normalized context values independently of native tables and invalidates gear and running searches when those fields change. It accepts armor only following the current zone boundary. `AZSARMOR` has no run identifier. An older armor message delivered after a new valid zone message remains indistinguishable if it otherwise validates.

The historical screenshot, price material, installed third-party source and generated data, and the independent expected-value CSV remain local. Reviewers can inspect the public references, SurvivorUI source, [parameterized verification script](../../scripts/verify_reborn_gear.py), tests, replay fixtures, and CI. The [input format and reproduction commands](../REBORN_GEAR.md#offline-verification) document verification with independently provided data. Treat earlier local observations as reported evidence until reproduced.

### Window design and native pause contract

The analyzer reuses SurvivorUI's `SurvivorUI_GoldButton` and the addon's font and flat dark/gold treatments. The tool uses an opaque panel, a thin border, distinct primary/secondary and selected states, a clipped scrolling viewport, and compact slot controls. Price, pressure, and set or acquisition changes have separate presentation. Window geometry responds to display size and UI scale while open. Native character-panel controls and tooltip ownership stay in the native integration path. The repository instructions now require these visual and input principles for future substantial UI changes. [Visual policy](../../AGENTS.md#survivorui-interface-design)

Installed `Run.lua` pauses when the native game menu or another recognized host menu is shown and resumes when they close. Its Escape wrapper preserves the stock menu routing while suppressing target clearing. Installed FrameXML handles a visible `GameMenuFrame` before checking special windows. It places the game menu in `DIALOG` and `GameTooltip` in `TOOLTIP`. The analyzer uses `FULLSCREEN_DIALOG`, local frame keyboard focus, and its own dismissal outside the special-window list. The native card picker observes repeated `OnKeyDown` without `OnKeyUp`. A short idle gap after the final Escape event keeps one repeat stream from dismissing both analyzer layers in the simulated sequence. An open selection list dismisses before the window. The temporary timer exists only while processing Escape. When opened over the game menu, a mouse shield intercepts clicks outside the opaque analyzer and is hidden on window close. The implementation leaves the native menu's visibility and the server pause state to the host.
